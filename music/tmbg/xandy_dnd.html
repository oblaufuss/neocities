X&Y GO D&D

Recently, I was asked to take part in a friendly Dungeons & Dragons game where each player got to design a team of two characters for an arena battle—TO THE DEATH. You can surely guess who I went for.

They did not win—I maintain that They were doing too well, and the others teamed up against Them—but they fought valiantly and died interestingly and were an absolute pleasure to play, so I thought I'd share their builds here. (Many thanks to my friend IgpayAtenlay, who knows far more about TTRPGs than I, and was a huge help in putting together these strategies.)

Note: These characters were built in DND 5e with some minor homebrewed allowances. They were super-optimized for (1) teamwork, (2) pure combat, and (3) dying spectacularly in a one-shot scenario. I have no idea how either of them would fare alone or in a long-term campaign, but I've tried to offer ideas for potential tweaks.

This build was designed to make it possible to fight toe-to-toe with a Level 8 Paladin superhero. York's job is to confidently handle up-close and personal confrontations with heavy hitters, buying Xander room to breathe while harboring a surprising variety of ranged capabilities himself.

Equipment and Infusions

Arcane Armor (Guardian) — "Indestructible, indefensible..."

Use plate armor (AC 18) for an EPIC MECH SUIT that skips the usual STR requirement. Comes with Defensive Field to pad his HP and melee-capable Thunder Gauntlets.

Pick just one Infusion to apply here, though York can switch between them after a long rest:

  • Armor of Magical Strength. If someone might try to restrain or shove him, you'll want this one.
  • Resistant Armor. Excellent if you know he'll encounter a specific type of damage that can bypass AC or CON saves.
  • Enhanced Defense. If you're really going for a cartoonishly high base AC.

The Chessmaster — A "highly misguided" custom shield/electric guitar.

Infused with Repulsion Shield, it provides a total of +3 to AC and lets York act as a bouncer when someone gets too close and personal. This is the focus through which York can mechanically produce magical effects.

(While it's less satisfying from a flavor text point of view, he can always switch to using the armor as a focus if something happens to the shield.)

York is quite versatile: Thunder, Fire, Psychic, and Necrotic damage are all represented here. Use Fire Bolt against the AC of squishy wizards who think they're standing at a safe distance, while the Wizard spells can target the dump-stat INT or WIS of any high-AC meathead chasing him down. Toll the Dead's 2d12 is quite high damage for a cantrip.

Booming Blade is less efficient damagewise than just punching someone twice, but the movement control part is valuable for teamwork (punishing them for trying to escape an environmental hazard or breaking off melee to harass Xander). I've also played York solo with this one subsitutued for Shocking Grasp—it gets advantage against opponents with metal armor and lets York reposition himself afterward without triggering an opportunity attack. Plus, very mad-science flavorful. KA-ZAP!

York can know more spells than the above, though, so here are some other spells that are either useful or flavorful enough to consider:

  • Cure Wounds. Good for emergencies, but actually less efficient than taking an action to drink one of Xander's health potions. Cast this at L2 or not at all.
  • Disguise Self. York loooves the concept of going undercover, but he's miserable at both stealth and deception.
  • Identify. Slightly expensive material component, but this just seems like an ability York should have despite his middling Arcana skill. He's a gearhead!

Strategies and Warnings

  • The difference between a villain and a supervillain? Presentation! Roleplay it up, people! York may not be mechanically "charismatic", but he is mechanically loud, unpredictable, and difficult to ignore. Talking is a free action. That makes it a resource, and York never leaves resources on the table.
  • Don't fall for your own hype. York simulates power through cleverness and consumable effects. His build makes up-close and personal violence lots of fun, but he's ultimately an INT-based character. Forget that, and the real heavy hitters will eat him alive.
  • Give John X. his personal space. Xander can take care of himself! Separate so you can't both be targeted by area effects. You're both ranged casters; you can support each other from a distance.
  • Sharing's great, sharing's neat. York only really has two important pieces of equipment to worry about; his third infusion can go to Xander. Magic items that don't require attunement are an easy one. (See Xander's equipment for a suggestion.)
  • Watch out for: darkness (darn nightvision-less human eyeballs!), environmental hazards that someone could drag you into, WIS saves (especially ones that block you from taking reactions, like Slow or Confusion) and AC-bypassing damage like Heat Metal or Cloud of Daggers.
