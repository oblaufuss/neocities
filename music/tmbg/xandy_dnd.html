X&Y GO D&D

Recently, I was asked to take part in a friendly Dungeons & Dragons game where each player got to design a team of two characters for an arena battle—TO THE DEATH. You can surely guess who I went for.

They did not win—I maintain that They were doing too well, and the others teamed up against Them—but they fought valiantly and died interestingly and were an absolute pleasure to play, so I thought I'd share their builds here. (Many thanks to my friend IgpayAtenlay, who knows far more about TTRPGs than I, and was a huge help in putting together these strategies.)

Note: These characters were built in DND 5e with some minor homebrewed allowances. They were super-optimized for (1) teamwork, (2) combat, and (3) dying spectacularly in a one-shot scenario. I have no idea how either of them would fare alone or in a long-term campaign, but I've tried to offer ideas for potential tweaks.

This build was originally designed to make it possible to fight toe-to-toe with a Level 8 Paladin superhero. York's job is to handle up-close and personal confrontation with a heavy hitter, buying Xander room to breathe while harboring a surprising variety of ranged capabilities himself.

Equipment and Infusions

Arcane Armor (Guardian) — "Indestructible, indefensible"

Use plate armor (AC 18) for an EPIC MECH SUIT that skips the usual STR requirement. Comes with Defensive Field to pad his HP and melee-capable Thunder Gauntlets.

Pick just one Infusion to apply here, though York can switch between them after a long rest:

  • Armor of Magical Strength. If someone might try to restrain or shove him, you'll want this one.
  • Resistant Armor. Excellent if you know he'll encounter a specific type of damage that can bypass AC or CON saves.
  • Enhanced Defense. If you're really going for a cartoonishly high base AC.

The Chessmaster — A "highly misguided" custom shield/electric guitar.

Infused with Repulsion Shield, it provides a total of +3 to AC and lets York act as a bouncer, no STR check needed, when someone gets too close and personal. This is the focus through which York produces magical effects.

(While it's less satisfying from a flavor text point of view, he can always switch to using his armor as a focus if something happens to the Chessmaster.)

Night-Vision Spex — "I see you through my spyglass, baby..."

York's third and last infusion can be used to supplement his sorry human eyeballs by applying Goggles of Night to his glasses. However, infusions can be applied to other people's equipment, and Xander might actually need the vision boost more! (More notes on that here.)


Attacks and Spells

Attacks:

Thunder Gauntlets — "That crazy bastard wants to hit me!"
York delivers a thunderous punch with one comically oversized paper mache metal hand.

Armorers get multiattack at level 5, so York can attempt two punches per turn for a not-too-shabby 2d8+8 combined damage. If a gauntlet punch lands, the target temporarily has disadvantage on attacks against anyone but York. Their apparent nonchalance belies the fact that they can only think of him...

Cantrips:

Fire Bolt — "People in the back, I'd like to ask you to disregard the fire laws!"
A jet of flame shoots from the shield's "headstock".

Toll The Dead* — "You're older than you've ever been / and now you're even older"
York plucks a single string, and the ominous note fills you with soul-sucking existential dread.

Mind Sliver* — "Like a volume beyond comprehension..."
York cranks a guitar dial all the way up and a screech of feedback seems to pierce your brain, leaving you disoriented.

Booming Blade — "You can't get away / You can't really hide"
York throws a super-charged punch with his Thunder Gauntlets, getting a second guaranteed hit if you try to move afterwards.

Click here for further notes.

(*) Spells marked with an asterisk are Wizard spells learned via Magic Initiate.

York is quite versatile: Thunder, Fire, Psychic, and Necrotic damage are all represented here. Use Fire Bolt against the AC of squishy wizards who think they're standing at a safe distance, while the Wizard spells can target the dump-stat INT or WIS of any high-AC meathead chasing him down. Toll the Dead's 2d12 is quite high damage for a cantrip.

Booming Blade is less efficient damagewise than just punching someone twice, but the ability to discourage movement is helpful for team strategies. I've played him solo with this one subsitutued for Shocking Grasp, which gets advantage against opponents with metal armor + prevents an opportunity attack if he needs to retreat. Plus, very mad-science flavorful. KA-ZAP!

Level 1 (4 slots):

Shield* — "You'll always miss / My big ol' body"
A temporary forcefield crackles around York the moment before a blow strikes, causing it to spark harmlessly off the surface.

Magic Missile — "Let's get those missiles ready to destroy the universe!"
Panels pop open in the shoulders of York's armor, tiny launchers swiveling towards their targets.

Thunderwave — "If the bass doesn't get you..."
York strums a gnarly chord and the earthshaking force of the reverb blows everybody nearby several steps backwards.

Click here for further notes.

(*) The spell marked with an asterisk is a Wizard spell learned via Magic Initiate. York gets one bonus use of this.

If York is living up to his full windmill-tiltin' potential, ALL his L1 spell slots (plus the bonus one he gets from Magic Initiate) will go to Shield. As a reaction, it can be saved for the hits he knows will land and hurt, and the AC bonus persists until the end of the round. When these slots are gone... try to have an exit strategy.

Magic Missile is here to break Concentration on spells like Entangle that York can't STR-save himself out of. Damagewise, it's barely better than a cantrip. Consider casting at L2 to avoid wasting a potential Shield.

Thunderwave is loads of fun in the less Giant-Killer-y scenario of being swarmed by smaller, more fragile enemies. Still, consider casting at L2.

York will know a couple more spells than the above, so here are some others I consider reasonably useful and on-theme:

  • Cure Wounds. Good for emergencies, but actually less efficient than taking an action to drink a Healing Elixir from Xander. Cast this at L2 or not at all.
  • Disguise Self. York loves the idea of going undercover, though his rather distinctive... self does his Deception roll no favors. Better play it causal and hope nobody asks him any direct questions. Xander's Transformation Elixir has the same effect, albeit for only 10 min.
  • Identify. Slightly expensive material component, but this just seems like an ability York should have. He's a gearhead!
  • Alarm. Complete paranoia is total awareness, as certain cult leaders have said. Keep an ear out for unexpected visitors while Xander gets his sleep.

Level 2 (2 slots):

Shatter — "...the treble will get you"
York strums a screamin' chord and the sound waves hit the resonant frequency of a nearby material, shaking the air around it to pieces.

Click here for further notes.

I'd reccommend saving L2 slots for upcasting useful L1 spells. Shatter does no more damage than Thunderwave cast at L2 would, in a smaller radius. The advantage is supposed to be that it's ranged, but York already has a lot of ranged options. Great sound-based flavor, though.

Other on-theme spells that York might know and use in specific situations include:

  • Magic Mouth. "Hello, you have reached the lair of John and John. Please, just stand there and keep screamin', and someone will be with you shortly." (You can do something similar, though less powerful, for free with Magical Tinkering.)
  • Aid. A support spell that doesn't require concentration offers a way to lend a helping hand.
  • Heat Metal. A damage spell that does require concentration offers another excuse to use the flamethrower-headstock.
  • Lesser Restoration. Practical to have on hand when your partner likes slinging around poisons.

Strategies and Warnings

  • The difference between a villain and a supervillain? Presentation! Roleplay it up, people! York is not "charismatic", but is loud, unpredictable, and difficult to ignore. Talking is a free action. That makes it a resource, and York never leaves resources on the table.
  • Don't fall for your own hype. York simulates sheer power through cleverness and consumable effects. His build makes up-close and personal violence lots of fun, but he's ultimately an INT-based character. Forget that, and the real heavy hitters will eat him alive.
  • Give John X. his personal space. Xander can take care of himself! Separate so you can't both be targeted by area effects. You're both ranged casters; you can support each other from a distance.
  • Watch out for: environmental hazards that someone could drag you into, WIS saves (especially ones that block you from taking reactions, like Slow or Confusion) and AC-bypassing damage like Heat Metal or Cloud of Daggers.

Arcana and Sleight of Hand skills come from the class; Performance comes from Variant Human; History and Perception come from the Rune Carver background, which a gracious DM let me tweak to replace the extra tool proficiency with wind instruments. Beneath all the fiddly little gadgets this build gets to play with, Xander's true job is to stand quietly back and look for the perfect opening to melt someone's face clean off. If his strengths are a little overly specific, York's jack-of-all-trades nature can pick up the slack.

Xander benefits greatly from the creative use (and possibly abuse) of item effects, so there will be several "check with your DM" moments here.

Equipment and Infusions

Accordion Bellows of Holding — "Can't just ask some perfect stranger what they're hiding in their accordion case"

Carry a whole laboratory's worth of gadgetry on the subway any quest. Let his hands fly over the buttons and switches, and out of his accordion comes whatever he needs. The hand held blunderbuss-bell is the focus with which he aims attacks.

Yes, I know.

This is the one infusion I'd set and forget. Fetching an item from hammerspace takes a full action, but Xander should only need to retrieve the kind of small, light objects he'd have in a normal backpack during combat. A DM will probably let you flavor text his cantrips and accessible inventory as also being "dialed up" through the accordion, though.

The "blunderbuss" element can be considered part of his alchemist's kit to give him the +4 Alchemical Savant damage bonus.

Shield of Mystery — "I'm going to die if you touch me one more time / (Well, I guess that I'm going to die no matter what)"

Maintain Xander's highly valued personal space with an infused Repulsion Shield: a total of +3 to AC, plus the ability to push melee attackers back out of range, no STR check needed.

Suggested Inventory

Ammunition — "..."

Easy-to-get damage-increasers:

  • Oil flask, 1sp. On a hit, adds +5 to any subsequent fire damage.
  • Acid vial, 25gp. On a hit, deals 2d6 additional acid damage.

Potentially useful poisons:

  • Malice, 250gp — "I guess you must be goin' blind / there's nothing left but the smoke that's comin' off the mind"
  • Ether, 300gp — "Little vials full of knock-out drops..."
Click here for further notes.

This is one of those "check with your DM" type things, but most should let you stack these effects with the projectile attack.

Xander has one last infusion I haven't mentioned yet: the Alchemy Jug. This boring-sounding magic object can generate 2 vials-worth of acid or 2 flasks-worth of oil a day. Both York and Xander have fire-based attack options, and 2d6 acid damage can stack on a L2 Catapult spell for a whopping 48 max damage in one hit.

The high expense of the poisons make them more niche, but both are inhalation poisons, which means even if your shot misses due to a DEX save (they duck and it breaks on the ground) or high AC (it breaks harmlessly on their armor), it still forces a DC15 CON save.

Malice is particularly powerful against magic users: most can only cast spells on targets they can either see or touch, and even if they have Lesser Restoration themselves, it'll cost them a round and a L2 spell slot to shake the effect off.

Ether is particularly powerful against individual enemies: until someone shakes them awake or injures them, they're down for the count, and you're out of combat turn order!

Still, I suggest pairing the poisons with a Mind Sliver from York to sabotage their saving throw, just to minimize the chance of wasting a resource. (If you do this, make sure to throw the poison with the Sling; Catapult forces a DEX save first.)

Odds & Ends — "Swimming goggles, penicillin, needle-nose pliers, lighter fluid, sasparilla, magnet, axe, woolen blanket, 1997 calendar, potato peeler..."

Props! Not a gimmick, just a clever way to put on a big show with small resources. Here are some cheap but useful items worth picking up at a shop:

  • 10 Foot Pole, 5cp. THE STICK!!! For poking potential traps and other things you wouldn't touch without one.
  • Bullseye Lantern, 10gp. For dividing an audience between People and Apes. Seriously, though, since neither of Them have Darkvision, this is a pretty high priority item.
  • Caltrops, 1gp. Deter pursuers through narrow passageways.
  • Crowbar, 2gp. Advantage to STR checks, since Xander is such a stunning physical specimen.
  • Tinderbox, 5sp. Sometimes it's good to be able to start fires nonmagically.

Strategies and Warnings

  • Play to your strengths, but make sure to play. It's tempting to let York's more well-rounded stat block handle most skill rolls, only jumping in when one of Xander's freakishly high bonuses apply. But either one may nat 1 or roll unexpectedly high. Mathematically, approaching a problem together is like approaching it with advantage!
  • Don't wander too far off.
  • Value your downtime. If Xander doesn't rest up, he'll burn out. Having a checklist of things to do and resources to stock up on between adventures should, paradoxically, make playing him feel less like an inventory-management problem in the field.
    Click here for some helpful downtime activities.

    • Use the Alchemy Jug on any day he doesn't need all his infusions active to refresh his acid or oil supplies, 2 units/day.
    • Make extra Elixirs with the spell slots he won't be using otherwise in his downtime.
    • If in a town, he can try busking with York to pick up some extra silver and possibly goodwill from the townspeople.
    • If in a city or somewhere with more shops, he can try selling or trading his extra Elixirs for poisons or other supplies.
      • Magic item prices are a little crazy to try calculating, but your healing elixir is slightly better than the standard 50gp one, and the others might fetch 100-250gp depending on DM ruling.
      • Honestly, the kindest thing you might do for your DM is have York help strike up some kind of deal with a major record company alchemist's guild to streamline this step if you're going to do it a lot.
    • Try to learn about the place! Xander's excellent History and Arcana rolls might help clue him in to what infusions will be useful to pick in the coming days.
  • Watch out for: opponents with high DEX saves, situations where York is surrounded by multiple attackers, and anyone getting within melee range of you.

Notes + Strategies

If your DM to let Xander buy these homebrew goggles, (1) DO IT, and (2) go swap out Inflict Wounds for Fog Cloud as the runic spell you know.

Spend time in the studio. Spell slots are expensive.

Note to self: 4 + 3 spell slots, not including the pre-prepared ones.

Suggested combo moves:

  • Get York to hit a target with Mind Sliver before Xander uses a L2 spell slot to hit them with Catapult, sabotaging their DEX save and making it unlikely that the expensive high-damage move will miss.
  • Or get York to hit a target with Mind Sliver before Xander slings a poison at them. (It's important to sling the poison rather than Catapult it here, or the debuff will apply to their DEX save again.)
  • If Xander is going to do something attention-grabbing, the turn after York hits the most dangerous player with the Thunder Gauntlets is a good moment. If they're tempted to attack Xander with disadvantage applied, it's more likely they won't hit anyone.
  • If York gets a successful hit with Booming Blade (forcing 2d8 damage if the target moves), that's a good time for Xander to Create Bonfire under the target (threatening 2d8 damage if they don't move).
