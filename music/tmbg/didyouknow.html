Pre-TMBG John anecdotes

  • Baby JL explains why Santa is real on TV

    From an email that John Linnell wrote to director AJ Schnack, on the subject of whether or not the Rabid Child music video could be included as a bonus feature on Gigantic:

    I appreciate you asking, AJ. And before I give you my answer I want to reveal to you that somewhere, there exists a really funny, cute video of me on Candid Camera when I was around five, explaining to Alan Funt why there is a Santa Claus.

    I don't know exactly how you would go about getting your mitts on it, but it was aired in I think 1964—perhaps 1965—and I am the second to last kid on the clip, wearing a stripey shirt and saying something like 'I think he's there, because I know he is, because I think he's there.' I was paid $20 for the appearance, which was a lot of money for a five year old in those days.

    Anyways, I'm offering this as a probably inobtainable consolation prize. There is no way I could agree to the Rabid Child video going down on my permanent record, Jesus FUCK no way.

    —Linnell

  • The mysterious Get Well Soon card

    From Gigantic:

    JF: You had an accident and had to go to the hospital in like, sixth grade? Or seventh grade?
    JL: Oh, and they made everybody write cards.
    JF: Yeah, it was this singularly bogus thing that because our 'schoolmate' John had to go to the hospital, we all had to write him letters. Saying, like, "Get well soon!"
    JL: Right. 90% of these kids had never heard of me or met me. So I got this giant pile of cards which... I probably threw them away, I'm sorry to say, John.
    JF: (laughs) Man...
    JL: I'd love to look through them and find yours. It was actually really mysterious on my end, too, because I got all these cards from people I didn't know. Like, who the hell are these people?
    JF: Yeah, I think my card said, like, "John Linnell. I don't know you."

  • Taxidermy/cannibalism, on 2018/10/27:

    JF: [Today] I presented my wife with our very first piece of taxidermy.

    JL: Yeah, we discussed this earlier in the tour. You weren't sure how Robin was gonna go for it?

    JF: It's definitely one of those "take it or leave it" things. I mean, taxidermy, come on, people. It's kind of—it's sort of a horrible idea.

    JL: Yes. And yet, how endearing. And wonderful.

    JF: So, I went to this antiques-parentheses-junk store, and found this very small piece of, uh... I'm not sure what animal it's supposed to be. It looks just like a tiger, if a tiger was this big. And we have determined that it will, uh- there's a special nook at the top of the stairs in the basement where it will reside. The big problem is that if you get something like taxidermy, you either, like- you're either gonna lose the friends you have or you're gonna have to move on to all new friends, who might... might be a little bit creepier than your current friends.

    JL: Right. But you could taxidermy your old friends, so you still get to keep them. Keep it all.

    JF: ... John, it's that kind of outside thinking that keeps this band fresh.

    JL: Think about it. Problem solved!

    JF: What's a little bit of cannibalism among friends!

    JL: (laughing) I didn't say—I just said stuff them.

    JF: Oh, I'd say eat them! With a delicious béarnaise sauce!

    JL: See, that's the basis of our collaboration. I say taxidermy, you say eat.

    JF: I would say pair that with a nice white wine from the Loire Valley.

  • Mermaid eroticism, on Studio 360, 2003:

    Host: Here on Coney Island, there's a big mermaid tradition...

    JL: Yeah. That, to me, is completely weird. I mean, the whole idea of mermaids as erotic is really weird, I think because... because they don't, apparently, have...

    Host: Well, to second base.

    JL: You can get to second base. Yeah, yeah.

    JF: [resignedly] We're talking about second base on PRI.

    JL: That's right. But that's where it ends. Pretty much.

  • Furby disasters, on 1999/09/23:

    JF: You know there was this gigantic earthquake in Taiwan just recently, and I was reading in the paper this morning that the Furby factory was destroyed—

    [crowd cheers]

    JF: No no—it's much stranger than that because as I was—it was in the business section and it was like, "could affect toy sales," you know, "the hottest toy." And all I could think of was the 20,000 Furbys that are all like in their boxes going like "Help me!" You know? They're all like ready to go and they're stuck under all this concrete talking to each other going like "Do you think we'll be saved?" "No!" "No!"

  • Getting called slurs, on 1988/03/03:
    [crowd shouting for Them not to skip Piece of Dirt]

    JL: I think we're going to have to do it. Because we don't want to get beaten up. The motivation behind a lot of our activities: avoidance of pain.

    JF: You know, the scary thing about being in a rock band is people continue to call you a fag much longer after high school than you ever thought... you would be called a fag. Last night a guy called me a fag. So anyways, back to the music.

  • Danny Weinkauf, stage bouncer

    When someone gets onstage with disruptive intent, Danny Weinkauf is usually the one to, well, remove them. Show recaps of 2004/08/14 and 2015/04/23 mention instances of this.

    JF on the Hard Times Podcast:

    "We did a show at Fort Lauderdale that wasn't a hardcore exclusive place, but they did the Sunday hardcore shows. It was a sort of weird indoor-outdoor place where we were on a stage that was six feet above the crowd for no particular reason...
    At some point, this guy jumped up onstage. He lifted himself- catapulted himself up onto the stage, maybe his friend helped him, but all of a sudden he's right in front of me and he's grabbing me... He's pulling me, and he's about to pull me off the stage, off the six feet. And our bass player Danny Weinkauf, who at this point was a seriously buff guy--he's got residual buff that will carry him through to the end of his days, 'cause he's just so fit. He's like a no body-fat guy. He did the full security guy thing and just pushed this guy off the stage directly, broke his grasp of me, and then this fellow fell into the crowd. And I just know, from looking at the faces of the other guys onstage, like, there's no way that he didn't break some important bones on impact... but I guess he just hobbled away to urgent care and, yeah, took one for the team."

Non-Architectural Fashion

John Flansburgh:

  • Wore purple nail polish sometimes in the 80s. (He hasn't said why he stopped, but getting tired of speculation on his sexuality might have had something to do with it, so don't be weird about this one.) Now, he wears clear polish to protect his fingers while strumming; silver polish showed up on his Sapphire Bullets persona.
  • "I remember doing an interview with somebody, some tabloid press, where it seemed like the only topic of the interview was that they were trying to figure out in sly ways whether or not John and I were gay... which was like,"(uncomfortable)"Okay. And the thing is, I'm from New York City, I have many gay friends, I'm a gay-positive person. I'm not going to make any homophobic statements or declare anything strange, so it was an awkward conversation to have..." (joking)"and is that because you're gay, John?" (sarcastic, resigned)"It's because I'm gay." (excerpt)
  • Frequently wore TMBG t-shirts in the 80s and 90s
  • Infamously wore a Northern Scum t-shirt which was obscured by his guitar strap to read "RTHERN CUM"
  • Wore the bright orange lining to a jacket on TV, which the washed-out tape colors converted to hot pink.
  • Loves cardigans; the most striking probably being the one that's patterned after the carpet from The Shining.
  • Has a WOMAN POWER pin
  • Uses a guitar strap with an owl pattern.
