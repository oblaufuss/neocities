Here's a short list of characterization guidelines and inspirations I try to keep in mind for X and Y:


There is no "straight man".

No matter how cartoonishly weird, impractical, or immoral one John's suggestion is, the other John will never ask "Why?". At most, they will come back with "Why don't we [idea that is at least half as bizarre] instead?" or object on extremely out-of-touch, eccentric personal grounds.

For example:
"We should blow up the moon."
"We can't do that. I haven't gotten to visit it yet. We could try Pluto..."
"Aw, but that's so far away, nobody would be able to tell. Let's not, and say we did."

Amber might object that a plan is unreasonably complicated, expensive, or morally dubious if she's about to point out that they've overlooked a ridiculously simple way to achieve the same goal. But for the most part, she thinks they're funny and loves leaning into the melodramatic aesthetics of what they do in a way that actively makes them worse.


They don't actually represent a coherent ideology, evil or good.

Our antiheroes (York and Robin in particular) are consciously feminist, anti-racist, pro-gay rights, pro-working class, and aware of how the existing social structures

X and Y might claim to be "anarchists", "communists", or even "fascists" just to scare people, but they have no real revolutionary plans for society—they're not that confident in their own leadership. The vast majority their schemes simply seemed fun at the time, got back at someone they think "deserves it", and/or simply let them continue doing whatever the hell they're doing instead of getting normal jobs.


"Scientist" and "Supervillain" are less accurate than "Engineer".

Xander's areas of expertise tends to be theory-heavy stuff on very small or very large scales: chemical engineering, biology, subatomic particles, computer science, astrophysics. His inventions tend to be more space-age, smooth glass and metal

York's areas of expertise tend to be practical-application stuff on more human scales: mechanical engineering, metal fabrication, high-power electrical systems, RF controls, and motors and vehicles of all types.

Amber actually has her own (more realistically focused) area of technical expertise: tailoring and textiles, dovetailing into some serious materials science knowledge pickied up while iterating on fancier versions of her super-suit.


Some goals for style and tone:

  • Atomic Robo, particularly The Shadow From Beyond Time and Other Strangeness
  • The Secret Science Alliance by Eleanor Davis and Bring Me The Head of Susan Lomond by Connor B.
  • Tintin, particularly Destination Moon and Explorers On The Moon
  • The Fantastic Four, particularly the Silver Age and Ryan North's run.
